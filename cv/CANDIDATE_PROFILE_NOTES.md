  - Coarse grid first, then finer steps around the boundary where failures outnumbered passes.
  - Logging: vehicle CAN from a main bus, the supplier's tool for radar detections and objects (needed their permission), and a camera, all on one time-synchronised logging PC. He doesn't remember the exact wiring.
  - Finding sent to the supplier: near the edge of the field of view, some targets were ignored until late. A supplier engineer came on site, the boundary tests were run together, and the supplier made a small calibration change.

  - Timing reference: when the alert or braking fired, they stopped and marked the target's and the ego's positions on the ground, measured the distances and computed the time from the speeds; this was cross-checked against the activation signal time and the speeds in the logs. Target speed was constant; the ego's actual speed could differ slightly from the spec at low speed over short distances.

All else equal except parking angle, the target reached high confidence much later; angle steps were then narrowed to find the border.
